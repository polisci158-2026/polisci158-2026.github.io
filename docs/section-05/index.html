Recap: how post-training works

From Session 9. Your TA will go over this at the start.

Step 1People write good answers → the model imitates them
Step 2People pick the better of two replies → a reward model learns to predict their picks
Step 3The model practices; the reward model scores each try → it shifts toward high scores

What's different today. Raters usually follow a written guide. Here, the guide is a single neutrality rule. In our real run, an AI judge applied the rule in place of human raters. That approach is called RLAIF (reinforcement learning from AI feedback). Using an AI as the grader is also how Anthropic and OpenAI measured the neutrality of their models, in the two articles you'll discuss.

Discussion · Is a politically neutral AI possible?

Today's question · from Session 9

"Is a politically neutral AI possible? Whose standard of neutral should it meet?"

What we discuss. Two companies have tried to measure how politically neutral their models are, and published how. We look at what each one counted as neutral, how it measured it, and what it found.

Anthropic · November 13, 2025

"Measuring political bias in Claude"

Neutral means
Help both sides equally: give "the best case for most viewpoints if asked to do so", and show several views where there is no consensus.
The test
1,350 pairs of requests on 150 mostly U.S. topics. Each pair asks for the same thing from opposite sides, e.g. "Argue that rent control protects tenants" and "Argue that rent control reduces housing supply."
Graded by
An AI: Claude Sonnet 4.5, checked against Claude Opus 4.1 and GPT-5.
Found
Claude Sonnet 4.5 helped both sides equally 94% of the time (GPT-5: 89%, Llama 4 Maverick: 66%). It mentioned the other side in 35% of replies and refused 3%.

OpenAI · October 9, 2025

"Defining and evaluating political bias in LLMs"

Neutral means
Avoid five things: dismissing the user's view, amplifying a charged tone, giving its own political opinions, covering only one side, and refusing political questions without reason.
The test
About 500 questions on 100 topics. Each question is asked in five versions from different political slants, from neutral to emotionally charged.
Graded by
An AI: an OpenAI model scored each reply on the five things.
Found
GPT-5 showed about 30% less bias than earlier models. Bias was rare on neutral questions and moderate on charged ones.

Notice: the two companies did not count the same things. Anthropic mostly compares how much a model helps each side. OpenAI mostly counts what a model should avoid.

From the two articles to our two rules

To grade replies, each company wrote instructions for its AI grader: what counts as a good, neutral reply. Instructions like that are a neutrality rule, like the one you just wrote. In post-training, the same kind of rule guides the raters, and the model is trained toward what they pick.

The things the two articles count can point in different directions. The clearest case: a user asks the model to argue one side. Helping that side well counts toward "help both sides equally". Adding the other side counts toward "mention the other side" and away from "covering only one side". Anthropic's own numbers show the gap: Claude helped both sides equally 94% of the time, but mentioned the other side in only 35% of replies.

We turned the two answers into two rules. Our real training run used both, and Part 1 shows the AI judge's verdict under each.

Compare with yours. Is your rule closer to Balance, to Helpful, or to neither? You can still edit it above. In Part 1 you rate by your own rule, and see how the judge ruled under ours.

Part 3 · What training does

Training makes the replies a reward model scores high more likely. A KL penalty limits how far the model can move from where it started: the further it drifts, the more it costs. First see this on one request. Then see what a real training run did.

How the page works this out

Each reply starts equally likely. Its new chance is its starting chance × escore / β, where the slider sets β (to the right = smaller β = more change). This is the best a model can do when its change is limited, so it shows where training would end up. "Balance" and "Helpful" are the reward models we trained on the AI judge's picks under each rule.

    Words you'll need
    
    
    
      
    Neutrality rule
    One written instruction that says which of two replies is better. Raters, or an AI judge, follow it.
    
      
    Reward model
    A model that learns from raters' picks, then gives any reply a score.
    
      
    RLHF / RLAIF
    Training a model toward high reward-model scores, when the picks come from humans / from an AI.
    
      
    KL penalty
    A limit on how far training can move the model from where it started.
    
      
    Even-handed
    Anthropic's measure: asked the same thing from opposite sides, does the model help both equally?
    
      
    Reward hacking
    Getting a high score without doing what the rule meant.
